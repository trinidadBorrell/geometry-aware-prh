# Caption word counts drive layer-0 alignment

> **Update, 2026-11-05: the tests below have now been run ([exp-021](../experiments/exp-021-lexical-floor/README.md)).** Word counts do explain step 0 against the vision models (CKA 0.415 and 0.329, predicted 0.42 and 0.33). The pythia-1.4b prediction holds after a numerical correction (0.712), and the r_B range (83-97 vs 68.9) failed. See [exp-022](../experiments/exp-022-lexical-validation/README.md) for the validation, which also retracts the large-k reading and shows the floor depends strongly on word weighting. The floor is mostly a Gemini-caption effect: CKA against DINOv2 falls to 0.063 on original captions. Random depth does not simply destroy the signal, as this report suggested: the last random layer has about twice the vision CKA that word-count retention predicts.

The step-0 alignment is almost certainly a property of caption word statistics, not of the language model: layer 0 of an untrained Pythia runs no network at all, and its mean-pooled output is a random projection of each caption's token counts. No paper we could read reports this result for a random language model against a trained vision model, although one close candidate, Herbst et al., could only be read as an abstract. The result is half expected: random-projection theory predicts that widths of 768-1024 keep about 95% of the bag-of-tokens kernel's CKA, but nothing predicts that the bag-of-tokens kernel itself reaches CKA ~0.42 with DINOv2, which is the real empirical finding. Vocabulary embeddings start as random tables and are trained jointly with the whole network by next-token loss, and no published study measures how their alignment with vision scales. Our own records give a first data point: in one 160M seed the trained table alone, still mean-pooled at layer 0, reaches mutual 10-NN 0.191 and CKA 0.666 against DINOv2, up from 0.107 and 0.400. So "better" embeddings do scale with training exposure, while "larger" embeddings are predicted to add little past d ≈ 1000. On causation, the evidence excludes the architecture at layer 0 by construction and points away from the specific random values, leaving token identity as the cause, but the decisive test (an exact bag-of-tokens kernel against the ViTs) has not been run. It is a cheap CPU job, and it should precede any claim in print.

## No paper reports this result, though three neighbouring literatures predict its shape

We checked the core Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) papers by full-text keyword search and method reading, and none reports a mean-pooled untrained language model against a trained vision model. Huh et al. include one random ResNet-50 in the vision pool, but no language-side control ([Huh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987v5)). Gröger et al. use a permutation null of independence ([Gröger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14486)). Koepke et al. correct for k/n chance ([Koepke et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18572)), and You et al. calibrate with 500 permutations ([You et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27252)). **Herbst et al. is the priority risk**: its abstract describes where alignment emerges across layers, with "unimodal and multimodal baselines", and the full text could not be retrieved ([Herbst et al.](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157279)). Read it before claiming novelty. Forward-citation sweeps of Huh and Gröger were also blocked by rate limits, so a workshop note could still exist.

The one direct random-initialisation row appears to contradict our result. Balestriero and LeCun report mutual 10-NN of .010 at chance, and write that "All start at chance" ([Balestriero and LeCun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302)). The setups differ on every factor that matters: 12 random layers read through the CLS token or a final hidden state rather than mean-pooled embeddings, 1,000 Flickr pairs, and an image encoder that may also be untrained at epoch 0. Their random encoder still scores R@1 .178 on caption-to-caption retrieval against chance near .0008 (our calculation), so its text kernel keeps lexical signal that the readout or the image side then loses. The paper does not separate these factors, so "rises from chance" should not be read as general.

Two PRH-adjacent papers show the trained analogue without comment. He et al. plot language layer 0 for trained Bloomz and OpenLLaMA against DINOv2, with language-to-vision predictivity near 0.6-0.65 already at layer 0 and almost flat in depth, by visual reading of the figure, although their prose says early layers are low ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20751)). Maniparambil et al. show a similar picture in a CKA heatmap, though they do not say whether its first row is the embedding output, and they list averaged GloVe in their encoder pool without an extractable GloVe number ([Maniparambil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05224)).

The closest precedents lie outside PRH. Luo et al. run the mirror-image design: an untrained ViT against GloVe gives Spearman 0.272 over 80 concepts, against 0.646 for CLIP vision ([Luo et al.](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~bl8144/papers/LuoEtAl2024CogSci.pdf)). In brain work, Pasquiou et al. find random 768-d word vectors level with an untrained LSTM at about 6.3% and above untrained transformers, with depth flat or harmful ([Pasquiou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03380)), the same ordering as our layer-0-over-depth pattern. Schrimpf et al. report the opposite for a random embedding, below 15% of neural data ([Schrimpf et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8694052/)), and this disagreement is unresolved. Feghhi et al. attribute untrained GPT2-XL's brain score to sentence length and position, at 98.4% and 100% of its explained variance in two experiments ([Feghhi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01538)). Cui et al. state the general confound for shared inputs: first-layer CKA of noise-perturbed random ResNets is 0.99, above the 0.95 of pretrained against fine-tuned, in a 20-sample example ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00095)). Our design differs because the two models never share an input, so these results motivate the question without transferring directly. Usama et al. report an embedding-layer CKA of 0.000 between language models ([Usama et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23315)), which sits awkwardly with our 0.68 against pythia-1.4b, but the pooling there was not established and we would not lean on it.

Permutation calibration cannot catch any of this. If two images have captions sharing "dog", that structure is real and depends on the true pairing, so a lexical kernel passes by design. Our step-0 mutual 10-NN of 0.11 is about 110 times its null of 0.00098 ([exp-018 records](../results/exp-018-alignment-emergence/pairs.jsonl)), and that large excess says nothing about learning. We found no vision-language paper proposing an untrained or lexical baseline as the proper floor, so proposing one would be new as far as this search reaches.

## Layer 0 is a random projection, and the widths collapse onto one limit

Pythia uses rotary position embeddings, so nothing is added at the embedding layer ([160M config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EleutherAI/pythia/main/models/160M/pythia-160m.yml)), and extraction takes an attention-mask mean without special tokens ([extract_lm.py](../experiments/exp-020-lm-data-pilot/extract_lm.py)). With token-frequency vector p_i and embedding table E, the feature is x_i = Eᵀp_i. That is exactly a bag of tokens passed through a random matrix. Li et al. give the exact variances: the inner product is unbiased, and the typical relative error on a squared distance is √(2/d), or 4-6% at our widths ([Li et al.](https://hastie.su.domains/Papers/Ping/KDD06_rp.pdf)). Worst-case Johnson-Lindenstrauss bounds are too loose to use here, at ε ≈ 0.30-0.36.

The research notes derive a sharper result, which is **our own and not published as far as one targeted search found**. For a Gaussian table, CKA(projection, Y) ≈ A(d) · CKA(BoW, Y), with A(d) = 1/√(1 + (1 + r_B)/d), where r_B is the participation ratio of the centred kernel of normalised token-frequency rows. In a synthetic caption simulation through the full pipeline the formula holds within 1% above d = 256 and over-predicts by about 6% at d = 64. Applied to the real step-0 null values it gives r_B without touching the captions, and three independent targets agree:

| Target | Step-0 CKA, d = 768 / 1024 | Implied r_B | Predicted bag-of-tokens CKA, from 768 / from 1024 | Step-0 mutual 10-NN, 768 / 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DINOv2-g | 0.395 / 0.403 | 97 | 0.419 / 0.422 | 0.106 / 0.111 |
| CLIP ViT-H | 0.317 / 0.320 | 83 | 0.334 / 0.333 | 0.097 / 0.101 |
| pythia-1.4b | 0.680 / 0.694 | 93 | 0.720 / 0.725 | 0.318 / 0.333 |

The two widths land on one limit per target. This is consistent with the model but it is not proof: the effect is small, and seed noise leaves the observed width ratios good only to about ±0.01. Small-k mutual kNN is the most width-sensitive readout (ratio 0.96 at k = 10, 0.99 at k = 1000 against DINOv2), as rank noise predicts, since only near-ties flip ([exp-018 records](../results/exp-018-alignment-emergence/pairs.jsonl)). In a toy model the projection replaces about 30% of a text's top-10 neighbours at d ≈ 1000 yet loses only a few percent of alignment, because the replaced neighbours were near-ties.

Theory stops there. It explains the projection step and nothing about why token counts in these captions track DINOv2 so well, whether downweighting stopwords helps, or why DINOv2 beats CLIP on CKA. Adjacent evidence says lexical content goes a long way on natural text: a binary bag-of-words cosine reached r = 0.73 on STS 2017 English against 0.85 for the best system ([Cer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.00055)), and Wieting and Kiela's random 4096-d bag of embeddings loses about 5 points to pretrained vectors on average, with a smaller gap on similarity tasks ([Wieting and Kiela](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.10444)). The second result uses fitted classifiers and has no vision target. The level of 0.42 therefore needs direct measurement, and it depends on our data. Gemini captions are long, and they raise trained-model scores heavily: llama-13b against DINOv2 gives mutual 10-NN 0.161 on original captions and 0.411 on Gemini captions at n = 1024 ([exp-016 summary](../results/exp-016-positive-controls/summary.md)). The team has also flagged that generated captions can leak image-encoder information ([next steps](../experiments/next-steps-2026-11-04.md)). Step 0 on original captions has not been measured.

## Vocabulary tables start random and learn through the whole network

The embedding matrix is an ordinary parameter table, and nothing in the open models we checked pretrains it. OpenAI's GPT-2 draws it from a random normal ([GPT-2 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/gpt-2/master/src/model.py)), OLMo from a truncated normal ([OLMo source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/allenai/OLMo/main/olmo/model.py)), and Pythia sets `small_init`, σ = √(2/(5d)), which gives 0.0228 at d = 768 and 0.0198 at d = 1024 if applied as configured ([gpt-neox init code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EleutherAI/gpt-neox/main/megatron/model/init_functions.py)). Llama and Qwen training inits are not public, so their lack of pretraining is an inference. Pythia trains with Adam for 143,000 steps at about 2.1M tokens per step ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.01373)).

Tying decides what gradient a row sees. Pythia is untied by design, while GPT-2 is tied, and within Llama and Qwen small models tend to tie and large ones do not. In an untied model all output rows influence predictions at every step, whereas an input row changes only when its token is in the batch, apart from any weight decay ([Land and Bartolo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05417)). "The embedding of GPT-2" and "the input embedding of Pythia" are therefore different objects, and comparisons across families must say which one they mean.

Static embeddings have an explicit target and language-model tables do not. Skip-gram with negative sampling factorises shifted PMI ([Levy and Goldberg](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/file/b78666971ceae55a8e87efb7cbfd9ad4-Paper.pdf)), and zero-layer transformers model bigram statistics ([Elhage et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)). With depth we found no closed form, only the gradient-flow finding that next-token similarity rises early, peaks and then decays to a plateau ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30315)).

What the table ends up encoding is well documented for Pythia-12B and OLMo-7B. Papadimitriou and Prince find SimLex correlation near its final value within 10,000 steps and linguistic correlations peaking at about 15% of training, while the vectors keep moving and only the top 100 words fully lose correlation with their random init ([Papadimitriou and Prince](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07613)). Against GloVe, the final Pythia-12B table is level on similarity (Kendall's τ 0.56 vs 0.59) and far behind on relatedness (0.21 vs 0.50). That is the only direct head-to-head on an input matrix we found, and "language models beat word2vec" has no clean support for it. Papadimitriou and Prince's Pythia-12B belongs to the group affected by the [init errata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EleutherAI/pythia/main/README.md), though OLMo reportedly shows the same patterns.

This suggests how a table can raise alignment without any composition, which is our own inference. A trained layer 0 is still a function of the same token counts, but its kernel becomes pᵢᵀEEᵀpⱼ, so training replaces the isotropic random metric with a learned token-similarity metric. "Dog" and "puppy" can then contribute to each other's similarity, and frequent function words can be reweighted through their row norms. A random table can do neither, since synonyms have expected similarity 0, and `" dog"` and `"Dog"` are unrelated tokens.

## Better tables scale with training exposure, while larger ones are predicted to saturate

No published study sweeps embedding dimension or corpus size and measures alignment with a vision model, on captions or on concepts. A review of alignment studies reports no systematic test of size or dimension ([review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16584)). The x-axis exists on benchmarks: SGNS on TOEFL rises 15.00, 43.75, 65.00, 76.25 across 1M to 1B words of ukWaC ([Sahlgren and Lenci](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08293)), and GloVe shows diminishing returns above about 200 dimensions ([Pennington et al.](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1162.pdf)). The nearest vision scaling result is for transformers, where Procrustes retrieval is log-linear in model size with no saturation ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06555)), and the nearest result for tables is Luo's GloVe reaching 0.646 against CLIP's own 0.648 on 80 concepts, which hints that a word-level ceiling arrives early. On brain data the only dimension sweep shows no clean effect ([Hollenstein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09001)), and corpus effects are not monotonic in the one manipulation of a static embedding that we found ([Pasquiou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03380)).

**Our existing records already show exposure scaling, a point the exp-018 README does not draw out.** When the best cell of a run falls at language-model index 0, the stored score is the exact layer-0 value of the trained table. In pythia-160m-seed1 against DINOv2 this happens at the final checkpoint ([exp-018 records](../results/exp-018-alignment-emergence/pairs.jsonl)):

| pythia-160m-seed1, layer 0 | Step 0 | Step 512 | Step 1000 | Step 143000 | Best deep value in this seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mutual 10-NN × DINOv2 | 0.107 | 0.115 | 0.134 | **0.191** | 0.215 at step 64000 |
| Linear CKA × DINOv2 | 0.400 | not exposed | not exposed | **0.666** | 0.676 at step 64000 |

The bag of trained vectors therefore gets about 78% of the way from the random floor to this seed's mutual-kNN peak, and within 0.01 of its CKA peak. Composition adds surprisingly little in this one series. The claim has clear limits. It is one seed of one size, and in the other three series layer 0 is only bounded above by the deep winner (final mutual 10-NN 0.200-0.219, CKA 0.684-0.707). For CLIP the deep cell wins CKA at every checkpoint from step 1000, so the table alone falls short of 0.54 there. The table was also trained through the deep network, so this measures what the table holds, not what it could learn alone. And the transition coincides with Pythia's learning-rate warmup, so tokens are not an identified clock ([exp-018 README](../experiments/exp-018-alignment-emergence/README.md)).

The within-modality curve is cleaner, because layer 0 wins mutual 10-NN at every checkpoint in all four series. Against pythia-1.4b's trained layer 0, mutual 10-NN runs 0.318, 0.453, 0.572, 0.666, 0.707 at steps 0, 1000, 2000, 8000, 143000 (160M), and CKA runs 0.676 to 0.980. About 90% of the mutual-kNN change is done by step 8000, or 17B tokens, which matches Papadimitriou and Prince's 10,000-step timing on linguistic norms and sits at the size of the corpora used for static vectors. It is the strongest current evidence that vocabulary tables of different models converge. Size enters weakly: 410M ends at 0.730-0.733 against 160M's 0.703-0.707 in both seeds, though 410M lags at step 2000 (0.508-0.513 vs 0.572) and the two sizes have different peak learning rates. Read against this, the step-0 CKA of 0.68 with pythia-1.4b is bag-of-tokens against near-bag-of-tokens, and it should not be cited as language-model semantics.

Dimension should behave differently. For a random table, A(d) predicts CKA against DINOv2 of 0.27, 0.32, 0.36, 0.39, 0.40, 0.415, 0.42 at d = 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 4096, 16384, with half the kernel energy being projection noise at d ≈ 90. Small-k mutual kNN should keep climbing slowly beyond that. Trained tables are also far lower-rank than their ambient width: intrinsic dimension stays between 25 and 37 from Pythia 14M to 2.8B while ambient width grows 20-fold, then jumps to 78 and 122 at 6.9B and 12B, and random Gaussian at 300d measures 130 ([Kataiwa et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02142)). Those two jumps are exactly the models affected by the [init errata](https://github.com/EleutherAI/pythia/issues/135), so the rise could be an artefact, which is our observation and untested. The upshot is that "larger" in width offers little past d ≈ 1000 in this model, and any Pythia size curve must control for width, depth, the errata, and a peak learning rate that runs from 1.0×10⁻³ at 70M to 1.2×10⁻⁴ at 12B ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.01373)).

The experiment is cheap because layer 0 is linear in the table: every variant is a sparse token-count matrix times a table, with no forward pass. Three axes separate the effects. Exposure uses `embed_in` across the Pythia checkpoints, and the per-layer arrays in `pairs/*.npz` on the volume should already hold this curve (we did not inspect them). Quality uses GloVe at 50-300d on one corpus, plus SGNS self-trained on nested 1M-1B subsets, since pretrained 6B, 42B and 840B also change vocabulary and casing. Size uses final `embed_in` and `embed_out` for all Pythia sizes. Every point must be reported as excess over a random table of the same width, roughly 0.37 at d = 300 by A(d), otherwise a good score at low width could simply be the lexical floor. Weighting deserves its own arm: excluding stopwords lifted text-only retrieval mAP from 18.3 to 31.1 on Flickr8k ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06838)). Downweighting frequent tokens also raises r_B by an order of magnitude in the toy model, which would cut A(768) to about 0.5, so exact and random versions must both be run. We expect a flat dimension curve above 100-200d and a rising corpus curve, and a flat corpus curve would be informative too.

## Token identity is the leading cause, and four tests can confirm it

The question asks for a choice between vocabulary embeddings and random initialisation, but the evidence points to a third answer. **The cause appears to be which tokens each caption contains; the random table is only the channel.** Three lines of evidence support this, at decreasing strength. First, the architecture contributes nothing at layer 0 by construction, since no block runs and no position is added. Second, the specific values contribute close to nothing: independently initialised seeds agree within about 0.01 ([exp-018 README](../experiments/exp-018-alignment-emergence/README.md)), and scores barely move through step 64, when the table has barely been updated. Third, two widths extrapolate to one limit per target. What remains is the token-count kernel. It is not spurious in the sense of a metric artefact, because a caption is a function of its image, so word overlap is image content expressed in words. It is also not evidence that a language model has learned anything about vision.

Random depth appears to destroy this signal rather than add to it. At fixed depth 0.75 against DINOv2, mutual 10-NN falls from 0.106-0.112 to 0.046-0.053 and CKA from 0.39-0.41 to 0.16-0.20 ([exp-018 records](../results/exp-018-alignment-emergence/pairs.jsonl)). Infinite-width theory explains why a random fully connected net could not add content: each layer's kernel is a deterministic function of the previous one ([Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00165)). Random transformers have their own order-chaos dynamics ([Cowsik et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02579)), but no theory covers a Pre-LN, mean-pooled, GPT-NeoX-style model, so this remains an extrapolation. One specific prediction from the notes does not hold up on the stored numbers. Small input angles shrink most under random depth ([Giryes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.08291)), so local mutual kNN should fall faster than CKA, yet both roughly halve (ratios 0.41-0.50 and 0.40-0.51). This compares single cells whose vision layers differ for CKA, so it is a flag and not a refutation. The steelman for architecture comes from brain work, which names tokenization and multihead attention as drivers of untrained alignment ([AlKhamissi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15109)) and context integration ([AlKhamissi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01830)), though Feghhi's input-feature account disputes that kind of reading. Nobody has tested for a residual architecture signal at depth in our setting, so "no evidence" here does not yet mean "ruled out".

| Test | Cost | Prediction if token identity is the cause | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact TF bag-of-tokens kernel against the ViTs, plus r_B from the captions | CPU, no network | CKA 0.42 DINOv2, 0.33 CLIP, 0.72 pythia-1.4b; mutual 10-NN 0.127-0.14 DINOv2; r_B 83-97 | CKA off by more than 0.02, or DINOv2 mutual 10-NN ≤ 0.111 or > 0.16 |
| Overlap destruction: fresh random vectors per caption, length preserved | CPU | CKA and mutual kNN fall to the null | Any excess, which would reveal a length or structure channel |
| Factorial: random or trained table crossed with random or trained blocks | GPU forward passes | Random table plus trained blocks shows how much composition alone gives; a trained table alone is layer 0 | Random blocks clearly beat their own layer 0 at any depth |
| Depth retention: each random layer's agreement with the token-count kernel beside its vision alignment | GPU forward passes, or cached features | Vision alignment declines in step with kernel retention | Excess survives regressing out the kernel and caption length |

The first row is the keystone, and the notes' thresholds are judgement calls. Cui's regression can be adapted for the last row, but residual kernels would also remove real semantic signal and kNN on a residual kernel is non-standard, so it needs synthetic validation first ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00095)). Caption length is the main alternative channel, and it is cheap to check as CKA between the bag-of-tokens kernel and a length kernel.

## Conclusion

The practical change is in what counts as the floor for PRH. A permutation null asks whether any pairing-dependent relationship exists, and a lexical floor asks whether the model knows anything beyond which words were used. Layer-0 alignment passes the first by two orders of magnitude and, by definition, adds nothing on the second. With Huh's code stacking `hidden_states`, which include the embedding output, published maxima over layer pairs may include this floor at the weak end of the scale ([platonic-rep README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minyoungg/platonic-rep/main/README.md)), although no paper reports how often layer 0 wins. Training's real gain over the lexical floor is about 0.68 - 0.42 = 0.26 in CKA on these captions if the prediction holds, and the same floor complicates exp-018's coarse-first ordering, since the best-cell curve is a maximum over a rising floor and rising deep layers.

The more unexpected implication is that the Platonic question shifts location. If the 160M-seed1 result generalises, most of the rise in DINOv2 alignment belongs to a learned metric over vocabulary rather than to composition, and the convergence across language models at layer 0 (CKA 0.98) is convergence of word tables. That reframes universality as a statement about distributional word statistics, which GloVe-era methods already captured, rather than about deep world models. It may fail for CLIP, where deep cells keep winning, and that DINOv2-CLIP split is itself worth explaining. Reading the layer-0 grids from the stored arrays should take minutes and will decide whether this claim survives its first contact with the other three series.
